The Problem
01 · The problem

The work was in the platform.
The conversation was not.

Six teams managing one deal over months. Each team communicating in different tools, with different people, leaving different records. Every handoff a context gap.

Email
BO → PM: NDA summary forwarded. Legal not included.
Legal → BD only: territory concern. PM unaware for 3 days.
!Legal joins Week 2 with zero context on the delay BO flagged.
Slack
Delivery hold flagged in #ip-requests. No platform record.
!Compliance raises same concern independently 4 days later.
PM → Legal DM: 3rd conversation on the same issue.
Jira / DMs
Ticket raised. Copy-pasted email context. 3 people missing.
Legal & BD DM: decision made, not documented anywhere.
!Undocumented decision resurfaces 6 weeks later. No record.

04
Hard Constraints
04 · Hard constraints

Enforced. Not advised.

The customer thread model has architectural boundaries. Not guidelines that erode under pressure.

✓
Started from inside an internal thread only
Never from a standalone surface. The internal thread is the prerequisite. Customer and stage are always known.
✓
External messages as selectable, editable context
The team curates what the customer sees. All editable before the thread starts.
×
Cannot start a second customer thread from the same internal thread
One per thread. Enforced architecturally. Multiple threads splits the record and creates lineage ambiguity.
×
Customers cannot initiate
Customers can only be added to a thread that already exists. Architectural boundary, not a permission setting.
05
Learnings
05 · Learnings

Three things
that didn't leave.

1
The hardest problem was the model of initiation. Where a conversation starts shapes everything it can become.
2
External as the default view was the most contested call. It turned out to be the most important one.
3
This was not messaging. It was binding — tying every conversation to customer, stage, intent, and outcome so none of it could exist in isolation.
